# ACV, TDP and velocity

## What %ACV distribution means
%ACV (all-commodity volume) distribution of an item is the share of total store sales, across all products,
made by the stores that sell the item. It runs from 0 to 100. An item in stores that make 60% of all-commodity
sales in a market has 60 %ACV. It weights stores by their size, so a listing in one large hypermarket counts for
more than a listing in a small corner shop.

## How TDP is calculated
TDP (total distribution points) is the sum of %ACV across a brand's items. A brand with three items at 80, 50 and
20 %ACV has 230 TDP. Over a period, Share Radar also sums TDP across the weeks, so four weeks at 230 TDP give
920. TDP adds up across items and markets, which is why Share Radar uses it rather than the maximum %ACV of any
one item (decision D2).

## Distribution share
Distribution share is the brand's TDP divided by the category's TDP in the same market and period. It is the
first factor of the share bridge: if a brand's distribution share falls, its value share falls with it unless the
other levers make up for it.

## Velocity
Velocity is sales per distribution point: value or units divided by TDP. It measures how well the brand sells
where it is stocked. Share Radar compares the brand's velocity with the category's, so a velocity index above 1
means the brand sells faster than the category average per point of distribution. With base (non-promoted)
units available, the bridge uses base unit velocity so promotions do not inflate it.

## Price and promo indices
The price index is the brand's average price (value per unit, or base value per base unit) divided by the
category's. The promo index is actual value sales divided by base value sales, again relative to the category.
A promo index above 1 means a larger share of the brand's sales came from promotion than for the category.
